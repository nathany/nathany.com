In late 2005, I picked up a copy of Agile Web Development with Rails and began dabbling. Drank the koolaid at Canada on Rails the following April.

It wasn't until February 2007 that I began paid work in Rails, and in May I landed a 10 month full-time contract with Power to Change. The Rails 1.2.x project was live for 4 years before being taken down due to software rot.

In July 2009, I joined Yardstick, to continue building out a Ruby on Rails 2.x app, while also maintaining their legacy ColdFusion app. During these three years, I've also been involved with @yegrb from the start, and competing in Rails Rumble and other hackathons.

At Canada on Rails I found myself appreciating the direction Rails was headed, with RESTful interfaces and decisions around what belongs in core. Likewise, I appreciate the infusion of Merbists who brought loose coupling and cleaner code to Rails 3.0, along with the support for unobtrusive JavaScript. The direction, community and ecosystem continue to be a strong point for working with Rails.
