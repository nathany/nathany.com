A *career break* is a broad term for stepping away from a career for any reason at all. It doesn't require permission. A *mini-retirement* is similar, but carries connotations of rest, leisure, and travel. A *sabbatical* is time-off arranged with an employer, with the expectation of return, and an emphasis on professional development or research. In the case of University professors, a sabbatical leave is often paid.

## How I got here

In late 2019, I happened upon [<span class="fab fa-youtube fa"></span>The Simple Path to Wealth](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T71ibcZAX3I) by JL Collins (Talks at Google). I subsequently read a number of books on personal finance, starting with The Simple Path to Wealth, followed by Wealthing Like Rabbits, Millionaire Teacher, and Quit Like A Millionaire.

I was working part-time and enjoying an extra free day every week. But I was starting to think about retirement, maybe early retirement, or even just "F-you money". I decided to go full-time at the beginning of 2020, but rather than inflate my lifestyle, I would invest the difference in low-cost index funds (ETFs).

### Fintech

In 2021 I began a new project working in financial technologies (fintech). So in 4.5 years, have I learned any deep insights that impacted my own financial journey?

No, not really.

The millionaires who need specialized software to manage their wealth are not on the same path as I am. They own companies and real estate investments, and are invested in a huge array of complicated financial instruments.

On the other hand, I have one globally diversified index fund, one mortgage, and some cash.

### Mortgage

After renting for decades, I purchased my first (and only) house in 2017 with 10% down. Fortunately I don't live in most expensive of Canadian 🇨🇦 cities, so it was relatively affordable.

My mortgage came up for renewal in late 2022, with interest rates that were difficult to stomach. Such is life, I guess. 🤷🏼‍♂️

I decided to let my investment portfolio coast, while diverting much of my income towards my mortgage. Partially because of those high interest rates, and partially because I was aiming to retire early, and paying off my mortgage first seemed like a pretty good idea.

When I first got a mortgage, I didn't expect to have it paid off until I'm 65 years old. That is still the case today, even though I've paid off ¾ of my home. The difference is that today my payments are only ~$550/month.

### House hacking

I have been renting out a room for much of the 8 years I've owned this house. The Canadian 🇨🇦 government doesn't tax rental income when [renting below fair market value][renting], which is what I currently do. My roommate is a sibling, so this works out well for him.

[renting]: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/rental-income/renting-below-fair-market-value.html
