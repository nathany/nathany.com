# Nathan Youngman 

My career in **web development** began **fifteen years ago**, back when dinosaurs still roamed the Internet.

JavaScript and CSS were in their infancy, social media hadn't been invented yet, and who knew that we would be browsing the web with mobile devices? The tools and techniques continue to change (MVC, ORMs, Ajax, Real Time Web, DVCS), and it is this **pace of innovation** that draws me to the web industry, even after forays into linguistics (CanIL 2006) and photography (TWU 2008).

Though _"climbing the corporate ladder"_ was never one of my goals, I was promoted from developer to acting CTO in 2005. This experience and subsequent project management roles only reinforced my **passion to create.** To be a developer.

If creativity was the only ingredient for fulfillment, I would still be building things with LEGO. The real motivator is to see _the value_ of what I'm building, both for my employer, and for the end-user. One employer cared _so little_ about user experience, they refused to inform customers of subscription renewals, even after _saving their bacon_ when they lost their merchant account. Those few days of development were critical, the **significance of my work** was clear.

In the search for _greater_ significance, I transitioned into the non-profit sector. What could be better than **benefiting society** as a whole? Though I was able to swallow the requisite pay cut, I did miss the longterm stability. The lack of developers to collaborate with made every big architecture decision a little more stressful, and the overall experience far less _fun_ than I had expected. While much of development is _"heads-down, in the zone, cranking out code,"_ **community** should not be undervalued, serving both for idea generation and maintaining sanity (sane minds, sane code).

Do you have a contagious passion for how your company is changing your industry, society, or even the world? Do you treat users with respect, prioritizing long-term success over short-term gains? Do you foster learning and collaboration among employees, and encourage creativity and innovation? This is the sort of workplace I am looking for.

We collaborated on architecture, through code reviews, and occasional pair programming. I was responsible for creating a booking system, adding e-commerce support (including taxation), company management (CRM), as well as multiple data migrations from third-party systems. In 2011, I accepted additional **project management** responsibilities, managing the tasks of our core team and several external contractors. I also served as the primary contact for a significant client, with offices in Montreal and Geneva. Still, I kept on coding, as visible in this [code_swarm](https://vimeo.com/35538422) of the project.

Now at over 400 wells, I can't imagine maintaining all that data in Excel.
