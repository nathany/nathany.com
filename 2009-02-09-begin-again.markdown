### putting down roots

It's been 3.5 years since my brother and I parted ways, leaving behind our apartment in Edmonton. While Terry began his career as an English teacher to South Korea, I took a short sabbatical in Europe.

Since my return, I have made my home in my father's house or by renting out a room. Those have been good times, but I am glad to be starting 2009 with *a place to call my own.*

My new suite is located 30 walking minutes from work. Though not huge, it feels open, and comes with plenty of natural light. 

After unpacking those things I had kept, I found a lot of necessities missing.  In the material sense, I am starting again...

### taking ground

Back in May 2007, I came to BC to work with HR in [Power to Change][PTC] for 4 months. Then IT hired me on for 6 months. After that, their [humanitarian arm][GAiN] asked me to work with them for 2 months, which became the last 7.

Working short-term has kept "what's next?" at the forefront of my mind. In a way, moving was me fighting that sense of insecurity, operating as though I am here to stay.

And why not? Things have worked out thus far. Call it *faith?*

Let's hope that I won't be moving for a while. This was my fifth move since coming to BC, which hasn't been the best for my sense of belonging. *How does one befriend the wind?*


### slow death

Career anxiety and the sometimes lonesome thirty-something life are but minor inconveniences on the grand scale of tragedy that befalls humankind. 

>"As we make our journey through this life, so many pieces of ourselves die along the way," writes [Rabbi Naomi Levy][levy] in *To Begin Again.*

What is lost is often deep within our soul. Dreams, innocence, trust, enthusiasm, hope...

I have carried a nagging sense of guilt since my [Grandpa passed on][Frank]. Friends and family came to visit and pray for him in the hospital. There was even a time when he looked like he would recover. One evening I brought some olive oil from the Holy Land, but then left without praying. I felt awkward, inadequate, I don't know. After that, he got steadily worse.

>Naomi writes, "Our guilt tells us that we are the cause. It allows us to feel powerful..."

Prayer really is for us. In that which we cannot control, our only responsibility is to leave it in God's hands, freeing ourselves from the outcome.

### flood

Naomi Levy's father was murdered when she was fifteen. In the moment her world was shattered, she lost her ability to trust God:

>"The omnipotent God I had always believed in no longer seemed compatible with the tragic reality I now faced."

We live in an unpredictable world. Tragedy befalls people every day, a tsunami shows no partiality. Why doesn't God prevent it? In the time of Noah, chaos reigned:

>"God looked at all that had been created and saw that nothing had turned out as planned... God decided to start over again with perfect order, with Noah and the ark and every animal two by two."

It was not long before we returned to chaos. 

God is clearly outraged with the injustices of the world. But instead of imposing perfect order, God *joins us* in the storms of life. He feels our sorrow, he knows our pain[^1], and carries us through.
